# Procedures: how a student defends themselves in Argentina

*[Español](../procedimientos-ar.md) · English*

This guide explains, with sources, **what stages the formal procedure has** when an
Argentine university accuses you of plagiarism or academic fraud on the strength of a
detector, and **where Descargo fits** in each. Spain and Colombia are in
[`procedures.md`](procedures.md), Mexico in [`procedures-mx.md`](procedures-mx.md),
Chile in [`procedures-cl.md`](procedures-cl.md), and Peru in
[`procedures-pe.md`](procedures-pe.md).

**This is not legal advice.** It is public information put in order. Every fact carries
its source and the date a person checked it by reading the official text. What could not
be checked is marked **to verify**. Every university also has its own rules, which take
precedence over the general ones: read yours in full before replying to anything.

A detector's percentage is **an indication**, not a sanction. To sanction you, the
university has to open a procedure and let you defend yourself within it.

## What applies (checked 2026-11-08)

**There is no national law on university discipline.** Each university sets its own:

- The **Constitución Nacional** guarantees «la autonomía y autarquía de las universidades
  nacionales» (the autonomy and financial self-governance of the national universities) (art. 75, inc. 19)
  and says: «Es inviolable la defensa en juicio de la persona y de los derechos» (the
  defence of the person and of rights in court is inviolable) (art. 18)
  ([text on InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/804/norma.htm)).
- The **Ley 24.521 de Educación Superior**
  ([updated text on InfoLEG](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/25000-29999/25394/texact.htm))
  gives the universities, among other powers, the power to «fijar el régimen de
  convivencia» (set the code of conduct) (art. 29, inc. l) and to establish «el
  régimen de admisión, permanencia y promoción de los estudiantes» (the regime of
  admission, continuation and progression of students) (art. 29, inc. j). It requires the
  students of state institutions to «respetar los estatutos y reglamentaciones de la
  institución» (respect the institution's statutes and regulations) (art. 14, inc. a).

So **the stages, the deadlines and the offences come from your university's own rules**.
What follows is the example of the UBA, the largest in the country. Use it to know what to
look for in yours, not as if it were yours.

## Example: the UBA

Source: the **disciplinary regime for students** (Resolución CS 2283/88, as amended), now
in the Código UBA, Libro I, Título 13, Capítulo F, arts. 501 to 523
([text](https://codigo.rec.uba.ar/codigo_uba/libro-i-normas-generales-de-la-universidad-de-buenos-aires-1/titulo-13-estudiantes-de-pregrado-y-grado/capitulo-f-regimen-disciplinario/)).

### What counts as an offence

The regime lists the offences and assigns a sanction to each:

| Sanction | Examples of offences | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Warning, or suspension of up to one year | Failing to show due respect to teachers or authorities; not observing the course prerequisites (*correlatividades*) | 513 |
| Suspension of one to five years | Insulting teachers or authorities; «Adulterare o falsificare documentos universitarios» (adulterating or forging university documents) | 514 |
| Suspension of five to ten years | Forging or adulterating «actas de exámenes u otros instrumentos, con el propósito de acreditar haber aprobado materia, curso o carrera» (exam records or other instruments, with the purpose of showing that one has passed a subject, course or degree) | 515 |

Sanctions must be individualised «en forma proporcional a la gravedad de la falta
cometida» (in proportion to the seriousness of the offence committed) (art. 503).
Offences lapse after one, five or ten years, depending on their seriousness (art. 519).

**The UBA's disciplinary regime does not mention plagiarism.** Whether the UBA or your
faculty regulates plagiarism in some other rule, and how, is **to verify**. If they charge
you with an offence, ask them to tell you **which article** covers it: it is a point for
your defence.

### The stages of the disciplinary inquiry (*sumario*, arts. 505 to 512)

1. **Complaint.** In writing, with «la relación circunstanciada de los hechos» (a detailed
   account of the facts). The university can also open the inquiry on its own initiative
   (art. 505).
2. **Opening and investigator.** Within 48 hours, the dean (or the rector) decides whether
   to open the inquiry and appoints an investigator, who summons you to give a statement
   within five days (art. 506).
3. **Investigation.** The investigator gathers the evidence and submits conclusions on «el
   mérito de la prueba y la eventual procedencia de una sanción» (the merit of the
   evidence and whether a sanction may be appropriate) (art. 507).
4. **Charges.** If there is no merit, the case is dismissed. If there is, you are served
   with the conclusions and have **ten days** from notification to submit your
   observations and offer evidence (art. 508).
5. **Evidence and closing arguments.** The investigator orders the evidence you offered,
   except what is refused with reasons. Once the evidence is taken, you have **five days**
   to present your closing arguments (art. 509).
6. **Resolution.** The dean (or the rector) decides «por auto fundado» (by a reasoned
   order), «evaluando la prueba con sana crítica» (assessing the evidence with sound
   critical judgment) (art. 510).
7. **Appeal.** A warning cannot be appealed. A suspension is appealed to the Consejo
   Directivo (or the Consejo Superior) within **ten days** of notification, with the
   grounds in the same document, and the appeal has **suspensive effect** (the sanction is
   not applied while the appeal is pending) (art. 511).

In serious cases, the dean can **suspend you as a precaution** for up to 60 days during
the inquiry. That decision is appealed within **five days**, also with suspensive effect
(art. 512).

**To verify:** whether those days are working days or calendar days. The regime does not
say, and the general rule that would settle it has not been checked.

## If the university does not respect your rights

Against the **final resolutions** of **national** universities, the Ley 24.521 provides a
direct appeal before the **Cámara Federal de Apelaciones** of the place where the
university has its main seat (art. 32). At the UBA, the Consejo's resolution on a
suspension appeal is final (art. 511).

The law does not set a deadline for that appeal, but one court has specified it. The Cámara
Federal de Apelaciones of General Roca, in its [Acordada 08-S/13](https://unterseccionalroca.org.ar/imagenes/documentos/leg/Ley%2024521-acordada%20Fed%20G%20Roca_0.pdf) (20-11-2013),
provides that:

- The appeal is filed **with the university body** that issued the act, not directly with
  the Cámara (if it reaches the Cámara, the Cámara forwards it to the university, and the
  deadline runs to the date it was filed with the Cámara).
- The university checks that it was filed within **thirty days of notification of the final
  resolution** that exhausts the administrative route (art. 25, in fine, of Ley 19.549,
  applied in a supplementary way).
- It is enough to **file it**: the grounds are not stated in that document (if they are, it
  is returned).
- Granting the appeal **does not suspend the effects** of the act (art. 12 of Ley 19.549).

If the university does not decide, the Procuración General argued in a 2021 opinion (case
FCT 7653/2015) that the art. 25 deadline does not run, but the limitation periods do.
Even so, **count the thirty days from the moment you are notified**, and see a
professional as soon as you receive the resolution.

**To verify:** whether those thirty days are working days, whether the other Federal
Courts of Appeals apply the same rule (only General Roca's was read), and whether other
judicial routes are open.
