# Procedures: how a student defends themselves in Chile

*[Español](../procedimientos-cl.md) · English*

This guide explains, with sources, **what stages the formal procedure has** when a Chilean
university accuses you of plagiarism or academic fraud on the strength of a detector, and
**where Descargo fits** in each. Spain and Colombia are in
[`procedures.md`](procedures.md), Mexico in [`procedures-mx.md`](procedures-mx.md),
Argentina in [`procedures-ar.md`](procedures-ar.md), and Peru in
[`procedures-pe.md`](procedures-pe.md).

**This is not legal advice.** It is public information put in order. Every fact carries
its source and the date a person checked it by reading the official text. What could not
be checked is marked **to verify**. Every university also has its own rules, which take
precedence over the general ones: read yours in full before replying to anything.

A detector's percentage is **an indication**, not a sanction. To sanction you, the
university has to open a procedure and let you defend yourself within it.

## What applies (checked 2026-11-08)

The **Constitución**, art. 19, no. 3
([LeyChile](https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=242302), last amended
2026-04-16), guarantees to everyone that:

- «Toda persona tiene derecho a defensa jurídica en la forma que la ley señale» (everyone
  has the right to legal defence in the manner the law provides).
- «Corresponderá al legislador establecer siempre las garantías de un procedimiento y una
  investigación racionales y justos» (it is for the legislature always to establish the
  guarantees of a rational and fair procedure and investigation).

This guide has not found a national law that sets the disciplinary procedure for students,
so **the stages, the deadlines and the offences come from your university's own rules**.
We read Ley 21.091 (on Higher Education), in whose text no disciplinary regime for
students was found, and Ley 21.369, which does require procedures "based on the rules of due process"
(art. 6, letter a), but only for sexual harassment, gender-based violence and
discrimination: not for plagiarism. What follows is the example of the Universidad de
Chile. Use it to know what to look for in yours, not as if it were yours.

### What counts as an offence

You are answerable when you breach «algún deber o prohibición estudiantil» (some student
duty or prohibition) of the Reglamento de Estudiantes or of the applicable university
rules (disciplinary regulation, art. 1). One of those duties is:

> «Reconocer el origen y autoría de las ideas y resultados tanto propios como ajenos/as,
> según las normas y convenciones académicas de cada disciplina»

(To acknowledge the origin and authorship of ideas and results, both one's own and
others', according to the academic norms and conventions of each discipline.)
(Reglamento de Estudiantes, art. 3, no. 6). It is the duty usually invoked for
plagiarism. Note that it speaks of **the norms and conventions of your discipline**: if
you cited according to them, it is an argument for your defence (*descargos*).

The sanctions run, from least to most serious, from a written warning to permanent
expulsion (art. 29). Among them is the **cancellation of the course** related to the
facts, «sin efectos de reprobación» (without the effect of a fail) (art. 29, letter e).
The sanction must «guardar proporcionalidad con la infracción cometida» (be proportionate
to the offence committed) (art. 30). The disciplinary power lapses **four years** after
the facts (art. 39).

### Two procedures: ordinary and simplified

The deadlines are in **administrative working days**: Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays
and the university's annual holiday period are not counted (art. 9). What the regulation
does not provide for is governed by the **Ley 19.880**, on administrative procedures
(art. 43). The authority of your unit, normally the dean, opens the procedure (art. 2).

**Ordinary procedure** (arts. 12 to 18), when an investigation is needed:

1. **Investigating officer (*fiscal*).** The authority appoints a *fiscal* who
   investigates (art. 13), with broad powers during the investigation period (art. 14).
2. **Charges.** If there are «fundamentos serios» (serious grounds), the *fiscal* draws up
   charges with a description of the facts «de forma concreta y precisa» (in a concrete
   and precise manner) and the rules that would have been breached, and you are given a
   **complete digital copy of the file** (art. 15).
3. **Defence (*descargos*).** You have **ten days** from notification of the charges to
   submit your defence and to request or submit evidence. If you request evidence, the
   evidence period lasts five days at most. You must specify which fact you want to prove
   with each measure (art. 16).
4. **Hearing.** A hearing of proposals, defences and observations, before the authority:
   the *fiscal* presents their proposal and then you or your representative speak
   (art. 17).
5. **Resolution.** Within five days. It must be «debidamente fundada» (duly reasoned) and
   assess the evidence according to «las reglas de la sana crítica» (the rules of sound
   critical judgment) (art. 18).

**Simplified procedure** (arts. 19 to 22): the authority may use it when it has been
possible to verify «la existencia y autoría» (the existence and authorship) of the facts
with written testimony, «documentos fidedignos» (reliable documents) or other conclusive
evidence (art. 19). You have **ten days** from notification to submit your defence and
evidence (art. 21).

If they open a simplified procedure against you on the strength of a detector report, ask
**how that report verifies authorship**. A similarity or AI percentage does not say who
wrote the text: it is a point for your defence.

### Appeal (arts. 32 to 35)

1. Against the resolution you can lodge a reasoned **appeal** (*recurso de apelación*)
   before the same authority that issued it, within **ten days** of notification. You can
   ask for a hearing to argue your case and submit supporting material, but not further
   evidence measures (art. 32).
2. The **Comité de Apelaciones** proposes a decision within five days, and the Rector
   decides within ten. That resolution «no podrá ser objeto de posterior recurso» (cannot
   be the subject of any further appeal) within the university (art. 35).

In addition, the act that ends the procedure is subject to the «control preventivo de
legalidad» (prior legality review) of the **Contraloría Universitaria** (art. 11).

## If the university does not respect your rights

The **recurso de protección** (protection action) (Constitución, art. 20) allows anyone
who suffers, «por causa de actos u omisiones arbitrarios o ilegales» (because of arbitrary
or unlawful acts or omissions), the deprivation, disturbance or threat of certain
constitutional rights to go «a la Corte de Apelaciones respectiva» (to the relevant Court
of Appeal). According to the Supreme Court's *auto acordado* that regulates it
([Acta 94-2015, consolidated text](https://www.bcn.cl/leychile/navegar?idNorma=1080916)),
it is filed «dentro del plazo fatal de treinta días corridos» (within the strict deadline
of thirty calendar days) from the act or from when the person had «conocimiento cierto»
(certain knowledge) of it (no. 1).

**What two recent Supreme Court rulings say** (according to press reports, not the text
of the judgments):

- In Rol 4309-2026 (2026-03-23, a fine on a lecturer at the Universidad de Concepción),
  the deadline was counted from when the person learned that the **reposición had been
  rejected**, not from the original sanction: the action, filed three months after that
  rejection, was declared out of time
  ([report](https://actualidadjuridica.doe.cl/corte-suprema-confirma-rechazo-de-proteccion-por-extemporaneo-en-sancion-universitaria/)).
- In Rol 8.270-2026 (a school, not a university), the Court ruled out that a complaint to
  the Superintendencia de Educación suspended or interrupted the deadline, and counted the
  thirty days from when the person learned of the sanction
  ([report](https://www.diarioconstitucional.cl/2026/04/30/corte-suprema-rechaza-proteccion-contra-colegio-por-inhabilitacion-de-apoderado-por-extemporanea/)).

The two rulings do not say the same about an **internal** appeal that is still pending,
and neither concerns a student sanctioned for plagiarism. As prudent advice, not a rule
drawn from a norm: do not assume the internal appeal extends the deadline; count the
thirty calendar days from the first notification, and see a professional as soon as you
are notified.

## Where Descargo fits

| Stage | What Descargo can do |
|---|---|
| Before submitting | `inspect` lists what the file carries besides the visible text |
| Investigation | `access-request` drafts the request for access to your personal data, including the detector's report (Ley 19.628). See [`jurisdictions/cl/`](../../jurisdictions/cl/pack.yaml), which explains the change of rules on 2026-12-01 |
| Defence (10 administrative working days at the Universidad de Chile) and hearing | `import-report` and `audit` help read the report and recalculate the percentage without quotations, bibliography or templates |
