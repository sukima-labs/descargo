# Procedures: how a student defends themselves in Spain and Colombia

*[Español](../procedimientos.md) · English*

This guide explains, with sources, **what stages the formal procedure has** when a
university accuses you of plagiarism or academic fraud on the strength of a detector, and
**where Descargo fits** in each. It covers Spain and Colombia; other countries will get
their own guides, each with its sources.

**This is not legal advice.** It is public information put in order. Every fact carries
its source and the date a person checked it by reading the official text. What could not
be checked is marked **to verify**. Every university also has its own rules, which take
precedence over the general ones: read yours in full before replying to anything.

## What both countries share

A detector's percentage is **an indication**, not a sanction. To sanction you, the
university has to open a procedure and let you defend yourself within it. What follows is
what that right of defence consists of in each country.

## Spain

### Which law applies (checked 2026-11-08)

- **Ley 3/2022 of 24 February, on university coexistence**
  ([BOE-A-2022-2978](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-2978)). It sets the
  offences and sanctions for students **of public universities** (art. 7), "without
  prejudice to the regulations set by the Autonomous Communities". If your university is
  **private**, which part of this regime reaches you and which part is its own regulation
  is **to verify** in each case.
- **The 1954 academic discipline regulation no longer exists.** Many texts and old
  university regulations still cite it. The BOE marks it as repealed "with effect from 26
  February 2022, by the sole repealing provision of Ley 3/2022"
  ([BOE-A-1954-17807](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1954-17807)). If your
  university cites it to you, that is worth a question.
- **Real Decreto 1791/2010, the University Student Statute**
  ([BOE-A-2010-20147](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2010-20147)).
- **Ley 39/2015, on the Common Administrative Procedure**
  ([BOE-A-2015-10565](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2015-10565)), which
  applies in general to the procedures of public universities.

### What counts as an offence

| Severity | Conduct | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Very serious | "Plagiarising a work in whole or in part, or committing academic fraud in the Bachelor's thesis, the Master's thesis or the doctoral thesis" | 11.g |
| Serious | "Committing academic fraud", defined as in art. 11.g | 12.d |

The law defines academic fraud as "any premeditated conduct aimed at falsifying the
results of an exam or of work, one's own or someone else's" (art. 11.g). The word that
matters is **premeditated**: a similarity or AI percentage says nothing about intent. It
is an argument for your statement, not a guarantee of the outcome. How your university
treats plagiarism in work that is not a Bachelor's thesis, Master's thesis or doctorate
is **to verify** in its regulation.

### Sanctions and limitation periods

- Very serious offence: expulsion from two months to three years, or loss of partial
  enrolment rights for an academic year or semester (art. 14.3).
- Serious offence: expulsion of up to one month, or loss of the ordinary sitting of that
  subject in that semester (art. 14.4).
- Offences lapse after three years (very serious), two (serious) and six months (minor)
  from when they are committed (art. 17.2 and 17.3).

### The stages of the procedure (art. 19 and art. 18)

1. **Opening.** The Rector's office opens it and appoints an investigator. The decision
   must state the facts, their possible classification and the sanction that could apply
   to you, and tell you of your right to make submissions, the deadline, and the option of
   mediation (art. 19.a).
2. **Investigation.** The investigator takes your statement. You have **ten days** to
   make submissions, provide documents and propose evidence (art. 19.b).
3. **Statement of charges.** If it goes ahead, it is notified to you and you have another
   **ten days** to make submissions, provide documents and propose evidence; then there is
   a hearing, also of ten days (art. 19.d).
4. **Proposed resolution.** The investigator drafts it within ten days and you have
   **ten days** more to make submissions (art. 19.e).
5. **Resolution.** The sanctioning body decides within ten days, with reasons. If it means
   to impose something more serious than the proposal, it gives you fifteen more days to
   make submissions (art. 19.e). That resolution "will end the administrative route" and
   must state the remedies available (art. 19.h).

The law also requires the person who investigates to be separate from the one who
sanctions (art. 18.b) and lets you be assisted by a person of your choice throughout the
procedure (art. 18.d).

### Your procedural rights

- To know the state of the file and to **obtain a copy of its documents**
  (Ley 39/2015, art. 53.1.a).
- To **make submissions** and provide documents before the hearing stage
  (Ley 39/2015, art. 53.1.e).
- To be informed of the assessment rules and of the grade review procedure (Statute,
  art. 7.1.g). Assessment must follow "public and objective criteria" (art. 25.1).
- To go to the **University Ombudsperson** (*Defensor Universitario*), which every
  university has, advises you on the procedures for making a complaint and may take on
  mediation, conciliation and good offices (Statute, art. 46.2 and 46.3).

If the act does not end the administrative route, the deadline for an appeal to a
superior (*recurso de alzada*) is **one month** (Ley 39/2015, art. 122.1). Which remedy
lies against the final resolution of a disciplinary procedure, and within what time,
**must be stated in the resolution itself**; see a professional before it runs out.

### Where Descargo fits

| Stage | What Descargo can do |
|---|---|
| Before submitting | `inspect` lists what the file carries besides the visible text |
| Before the accusation or during the investigation | `access-request` drafts the request for the detector's report and the data processed (GDPR and LOPDGDD) |
| Submissions and statement of charges | `import-report` and `audit` help read the report and recalculate the percentage without quotations, bibliography or templates |

## Colombia

### What applies (checked 2026-11-08)

In the rulings cited here, the student disciplinary regime is set by each institution in
its **student regulations** (*reglamento estudiantil*), within its university autonomy.
Whether a specific national rule also exists is **to verify**. That autonomy has a limit
set by the Constitutional Court:

> "in order to protect the right to education and to prevent university autonomy from
> turning into arbitrariness, imposing a disciplinary sanction must be preceded by
> procedural stages that guarantee the minimum elements of due process"

(Ruling [T-087 of 2020](https://www.corteconstitucional.gov.co/relatoria/2020/t-087-20.htm), para. 54.)

### The seven minimums of due process

The Court has repeated them thus (T-087/20, para. 55); the university must "at least"
guarantee:

1. **Formal notice of the opening** of the proceeding to the person accused.
2. **The charges**, oral or written, with the conduct, the offences and the regulation
   provisions that establish them, and their provisional classification.
3. **Transfer of each and every piece of evidence** that supports the charges.
4. **A period to put forward your defence** (*descargos*), orally or in writing, to
   contest the evidence against you and to provide your own.
5. **A final decision**, in a reasoned and consistent act.
6. **A sanction proportionate** to the facts.
7. **The possibility of contesting through the appropriate remedies** each decision.

These minimums "do not apply in the same terms nor with the same rigour" as in a court
case (T-087/20, para. 54).

The Court adds that offences must be **established beforehand** and that sanctions may
not be disproportionate (T-087/20, para. 56), and that universities may regulate conduct
such as "plagiarism or fraud" (T-290/23, para. 89, quoting the Court's own case law). If
your university's regulation does not define the offence you are accused of, that is a
point for your statement of defence.

### If the university does not respect those minimums

The **tutela action** (art. 86 of the Constitution and Decreto 2591 of 1991) is
available in university disciplinary proceedings, but requires standing, **immediacy**
and **subsidiarity**, that is, first exhausting the university's own remedies
([T-290 of 2023](https://www.corteconstitucional.gov.co/relatoria/2023/t-290-23.htm), para. 65 and 66).
That ruling decides two specific cases; only the doctrine is cited here, not the people.

### To verify

- The names and deadlines in each university's regulation ("descargos", "disciplinary
  committee", "faculty council"…): they come from **its** regulation, not from a general
  rule, and you need to read yours.
- Whether there is an office equivalent to the Spanish University Ombudsperson, and what
  it is called at each institution.
- Ley 30 of 1992 (university autonomy): its official text could not be read from this
  machine, so this guide does not cite it.

### Where Descargo fits

| Stage | What Descargo can do |
|---|---|
| Evidence (minimum 3) | `access-request` asks the university for the detector's report and the data processed (Ley 1581 of 2012 and Ley 1755 of 2015) |
| Defence (minimum 4) | `import-report` and `audit` help read the report and recalculate the percentage |

## How this was checked

The texts of Ley 3/2022, Real Decreto 1791/2010, Ley 39/2015 and the two rulings were
downloaded from the official sources (the BOE and the Constitutional Court's reporter) on

document's date and the current text in the BOE or the reporter before relying on it.
